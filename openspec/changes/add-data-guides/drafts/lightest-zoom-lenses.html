The lightest zoom lenses, ranked by mount

A zoom you forget is in the bag. Here's who makes one.

The lightest zoom lens you can buy new is the Panasonic LUMIX G Vario 12-32mm F3.5-5.6 O.I.S., at 70 g. That's less than half the weight of most phones. For full-frame, the lightest is the Panasonic LUMIX S 18-40mm F4.5-6.3, at 155 g.

Of the five lightest zooms on sale, three are power zooms, with PZ or EZ in the name. A motor does the zooming, so the barrel can tuck away when the camera's off.

Does sensor size decide how heavy a zoom is?

Mostly, yes. Here's the lightest zoom for each mount, split by sensor size where a mount takes two.

Smaller sensor, smaller glass. Micro Four Thirds wins at 70 g, while Fujifilm's medium-format GF35-70mm needs 390 g.

But full-frame is closer than you'd think. Panasonic's LUMIX S 18-40mm is only 20 g heavier than Nikon's DX 16-50mm, a lens made for a smaller sensor.

What do you give up for a light zoom?

Speed, mostly. Every lens in the first table starts at f/3.5 or slower, and gets darker as you zoom in.

And reach. The lightest full-frame zooms stop at 40 to 60mm, so think of them as a standard prime that can stretch a little.

Want f/2.8 all the way through? The lightest we list is the Sigma 10-18mm f/2.8 DC DN for Fujifilm X, at 250 g. That's more than three times the 12-32mm.

What I'd put on my X-S20

Full disclosure: I shoot with a Fujifilm X-S20, and I really enjoy it. The lightest zoom it takes is Fujifilm's new XC 13-33mm f/3.5-6.3 OIS, at 125 g.

How we weighed them

Weights are each maker's published figure for the lens. We count every zoom on sale in our database, 268 in all across 8 mounts, from first-party and third-party makers. A lens made for two mounts counts once for each.

Discontinued lenses and lenses that are announced but not on sale yet are left out. Leica M has one zoom in our list, a manual Laowa, so it sits this one out.
