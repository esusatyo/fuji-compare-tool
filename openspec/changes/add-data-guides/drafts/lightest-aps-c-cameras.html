The lightest APS-C cameras, ranked

Ten years of camera launches, one remarkably stubborn number.

The lightest APS-C camera you can buy new is the Fujifilm X-M5, at 355 g with battery and card. The Canon EOS R100 is 1 g behind. That's a paperclip.

Have APS-C cameras got lighter in 10 years?

Not really. Here's the lightest new APS-C camera from each year since 2016, colored by brand.

Every winner lands between 337 and 405 g. That's roughly a full can of soda, every single year.

The lightest of the decade is the Fujifilm X-E3 from 2017, at 337 g. Nothing since has beaten it.

And the Fujifilm X-M1 from 2013 weighed just 280 g. Progress, it turns out, went into autofocus and video, not the scales.

Which brand makes the lightest APS-C cameras?

Fujifilm, mostly. It had the lightest launch in 6 of the last 10 years, including five in a row from 2016 to 2020.

Then the others noticed. Sony took 2021 with the ZV-E10, Nikon took 2022 with the Z30, and Canon took 2023 and 2025 with the EOS R100 and EOS R50 V.

Fujifilm grabbed the title back in 2024 with the X-M5. Canon is the one to watch: two wins in three years, both on cameras under $800.

Full disclosure: I shoot with a Fujifilm X-S20, and I really enjoy it. At 491 g it's nowhere near this list. A proper grip and in-body stabilization weigh something.

Light isn't the same as small

The X-M5 and EOS R100 weigh almost exactly the same. But the R100 is 1.8× as deep, thanks to its chunky grip.

How we weighed them

Weights are each maker's published figure with battery and memory card. We count every APS-C mirrorless camera in our database from Canon, Fujifilm, Leica, Nikon, Sigma and Sony, 65 in all.

Canon's retired EOS M cameras aren't in the database, so they aren't in the chart. No new APS-C cameras have launched in 2026 so far.
