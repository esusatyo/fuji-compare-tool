Who makes all those lenses?

The camera makers build the backbone. Everyone else builds the crowd.

On Sony E, Nikon Z and Fujifilm X, other makers outnumber the camera maker by roughly three to five to one. On L-Mount and Micro Four Thirds, it's closer to even, because several camera makers share each mount.

Why does Sony E have so many?

A fifteen-year head start. Sony opened the E-mount specifications to other lens makers in 2011, free of charge, back when the mount was only on its tiny NEX cameras.

The crowd showed up: Sigma (47), Laowa (39), Samyang (35), Viltrox (30). Sony E now has 166 third-party autofocus lenses, more than any other mount.

Why does Canon RF trail?

Canon RF has 83 lenses from other makers, but only 16 of them autofocus. Sony E has 166.

Every one of those 16 arrived in 2024 or later, starting with Sigma lenses made under license from Canon, and all of them are for Canon's APS-C bodies. The other 67 are manual focus. The biggest third-party maker on RF is Laowa, with 35 lenses, and not one of them autofocuses.

Is more always better?

Not really. L-Mount has 130 lenses, and 77 come from Leica, Panasonic and Sigma, three camera makers who share the mount through the L-Mount Alliance.

Fujifilm GFX has just 18 lenses, every one by Fujifilm. Leica M has 80, 57 of them from other makers, led by Voigtländer with 29, and none of those autofocus. It's a rangefinder mount; that's rather the point.

My own X-S20 sits on Fujifilm X: 154 lenses, about three in four from other makers. My favorite is still Fujifilm's own XC 15-45mm.

How we counted

We count every current lens in our database for each mirrorless mount, 1196 in all. A lens sold for several mounts counts once for each. "From the camera maker" means a company that builds cameras for that mount.

Discontinued lenses, lenses announced but not on sale yet, and adapted lenses (Canon EF on RF, for example) aren't counted. Our list of budget manual-focus lenses is broad but may not be complete.
