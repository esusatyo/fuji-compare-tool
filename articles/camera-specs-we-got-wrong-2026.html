The camera specs we got wrong (and why it happens)

We checked every spec against the maker. The maker won. Often.

By Compare Camera Specs · Published 18 November 2026

We checked 1,369 cameras and lenses against their makers' own spec pages. Among the misses: a Canon EOS R1 listed 455 g too heavy, four cameras listed without the viewfinder they actually have, and a camera wearing its sibling's dimensions.

Spec sites copy from each other. So did we. Here's what turned up when we went back to the source, and the patterns behind it, so you can spot them elsewhere.

1. The phantom 455 grams

We had Canon's flagship EOS R1 at 1,570 g. Canon says 1,115 g with battery and card. That's a whole prime lens of difference.

See the corrected numbers in the EOS R1 vs EOS R3 comparison.

2. Hand-me-down specs

The classic: a new model inheriting its older sibling's numbers. The EOS R50 V carried the EOS R50's dimensions. In reality it's 45.2 mm deep, not 68.8 mm.

Same story for the OM-5 II (it had the OM-5's depth) and Sony's FE 16-35mm GM II, which carried the first GM's length of 121.6 mm. Sony lists 111.5 mm.

See how different the R50 V really is in the EOS R50 V vs EOS R50 size comparison.

3. The viewfinders that vanished

Four cameras were listed with no viewfinder at all: Canon's EOS R50 and EOS R100, and Fujifilm's X-T100 and X-T200. All four have an electronic viewfinder, as Canon and Fujifilm list.

The X-T100 was also 54 g light: Fujifilm says 448 g, not 394. Compare the EOS R50 and EOS R100, or the X-T100 and X-T200.

4. Weather sealing, both ways

We had the Fujifilm X-T50 down as weather-sealed. Fujifilm never says so, and it isn't.

The Canon EOS R8 went the other way: we had it unsealed, but Canon lists "weather-resistant construction". See the X-T50 vs X100V and EOS R8 vs EOS R10 comparisons.

5. Hood on, zoomed out, battery out

Some numbers aren't wrong, just measured differently. Leica's Summilux-M 28mm f/1.4 was 81 mm long with its hood on; without it it's 67 mm.

Fujifilm's XF 55-200mm was listed at 175 mm, its length zoomed all the way out. At rest it's 118 mm. And the Olympus E-PL5 was 279 g body only; with battery and card it's 325 g, the way we list every camera.

6. Right lens, wrong mount

Third-party lenses are made for several mounts, and the sizes differ. 19 of the 22 Sigma L-Mount lenses we checked were carrying their Sony E-mount dimensions.

Sigma’s spec tables list each mount separately, and the L-Mount versions are usually a couple of millimeters shorter. Browse the corrected L-Mount lenses.

How to spot a bad spec

  • Two models, same numbers? Suspicious. New bodies rarely match old ones to the millimeter.
  • Check what's included. Battery and card? Hood? Zoomed in or out?
  • Go to the maker. If the maker doesn't claim it (weather sealing, especially), don't believe it.

Nobody's safe. Fujifilm, the brand I shoot with, had 19 mismatches across its first 13 cameras. Even my own X-S20 was 0.6 mm too short and missing its UHS-II card support.

What we do now

Every camera and lens we've checked now links to its maker's own spec page, shown under References in the comparison tool. That's 1,369 so far, and counting. How we source our data.
